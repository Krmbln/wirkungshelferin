# US university / academic centres: impact measurement and theory-of-change methods

Scope: methodological, context-independent, freely available material that a small nonprofit could use for our 7-step guide (0 project context, 1 target groups, 2 problem & root causes, 3 ToC incl. assumptions, 4 indicators, 5 data collection methods, 6 data collection plan). Existing catalogue for comparison: PHINEO Kursbuch Wirkung, Aspen ToC facilitator guide, PHINEO Wirkungsrad, IRIS+/IMP (incl. "Using self-reported data"), SRS 2014, SVI Standards 2025 draft, Matatihi SROI guide.

Method note: about 18 tool calls. The direct PDFs for IPA Goldilocks ToC and ActKnowledge "ToC Basics" were downloaded and text-checked. Several institutional pages (Urban Institute, HBS working-paper PDF) returned 403/404 to the fetcher, so some details below come from search snippets or secondary hosts and are flagged.

## Which sources exist, what they say, and where to get them

### Takeaway
The most useful US academic material for a small, non-expert project is (a) Gugerty & Karlan / IPA's "right-fit" CART principles plus the free Goldilocks Toolkit, (b) Ebrahim & Rangan's contingency framework, which says many organisations should NOT measure long-term impact, (c) ActKnowledge/Center for Theory of Change "ToC Basics", with its 3 quality criteria and the 4-part indicator formula, and (d) the Urban Institute outcome-indicator sets plus the UW-Extension and Kellogg logic-model guides as free templates. Impact-Weighted Accounts (HBS) and J-PAL's RCT material do not fit a 1-hour-per-week user.

### Cited Findings

**1. Gugerty & Karlan, "Ten Reasons Not to Measure Impact—and What to Do Instead" (SSIR, Summer 2018). Free.**
- Authors are Mary Kay Gugerty (University of Washington) and Dean Karlan (Yale/IPA). The article is free to read. — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ten_reasons_not_to_measure_impact_and_what_to_do_instead)
- The ten reasons fall into four groups: Not the right tool; Not now (program design not ready, implementation not ready, too late); Not feasible (resources too limited, indirect effects hard to identify, setting too chaotic, implemented at too high a level); Not worth it (we already know the answer, no generalisable knowledge gain). — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ten_reasons_not_to_measure_impact_and_what_to_do_instead)
- "Not now: program design isn't ready": without a tested theory of change and validated assumptions, impact evaluation is premature. Collect monitoring data first. — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ten_reasons_not_to_measure_impact_and_what_to_do_instead)
- "Resources too limited": in small programmes the impact will be undetectable or ambiguous. Monitoring implementation is sufficient. — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ten_reasons_not_to_measure_impact_and_what_to_do_instead)
- "We already know the answer": existing evidence may be enough. Check that it applies to your context instead of repeating studies. — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ten_reasons_not_to_measure_impact_and_what_to_do_instead)
- The CART principles:
  - Credible: "Collect high-quality data and analyze them accurately".
  - Actionable: "Collect data you can commit to use".
  - Responsible: "Ensure that the benefits of data collection outweigh the costs", including the burden on respondents.
  - Transportable: "Collect data that generate knowledge for other programs".
  — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ten_reasons_not_to_measure_impact_and_what_to_do_instead)
- The underlying book is *The Goldilocks Challenge: Right-Fit Evidence for the Social Sector* (Oxford University Press). — [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/34819). I could not verify free availability, so treat it as likely paywalled.

**2. IPA (Innovations for Poverty Action) Goldilocks Toolkit / Right-Fit Evidence. Free.**
- The toolkit applies the CART principles to data collection, draws on lessons from more than a dozen nonprofits and social businesses, and is meant for "organizations of all sizes". It was built with the Center for Effective Global Action (UC Berkeley). — [S4YE summary](https://www.s4ye.org/node/1726); [FSN Network](https://fsnnetwork.org/resource/goldilocks-toolkit)
- IPA's Right-Fit Evidence unit builds on the Gugerty & Karlan book and works on "cost-effective, appropriately-sized" M&E systems. — [IPA news](https://poverty-action.org/news/devex-ipas-right-fit-monitoring-and-evaluation-program)
- The "Goldilocks Toolkit: Theory of Change" was released 5 January 2016. — [landing page](https://poverty-action.org/publication/goldilocks-toolkit-theory-change); [PDF](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/publications/Goldilocks-Toolkit-Theory-of-Change_1.pdf)
- From the PDF text (checked directly):
  - It defines assumptions as "the conditions that have to hold for a certain part of a program to work as expected". They are drawn as the arrows between elements.
  - It names two categories of assumptions: activity→output (implementation) and output→impact.
  - It separates assumptions from risks, which are "factors outside of the organization's control".
  - Links that rest on many assumptions "are also candidates for focused data collection".
  - It says to collect only data that relates directly to the ToC, and warns against collecting data "just in case" or because it would be "nice to know".
  — [PDF](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/publications/Goldilocks-Toolkit-Theory-of-Change_1.pdf)
- The ToC PDF says it is not a complete ToC manual. It covers basics plus CART and points to a follow-on module, "Monitoring with the CART". — [PDF](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/publications/Goldilocks-Toolkit-Theory-of-Change_1.pdf)

**3. Ebrahim & Rangan contingency framework (Harvard Business School, 2010 working paper, later "What Impact?").**
- *The Limits of Nonprofit Impact: A Contingency Framework for Measuring Social Performance*, Alnoor Ebrahim and V. Kasturi Rangan, HBS Social Enterprise Initiative, Working Paper 10-099 (2010). — [RePEc listing](https://ideas.repec.org/r/hbs/wpaper/10-099.html); [MandE summary](https://mande.co.uk/2010/uncategorized/the-limits-of-nonprofit-impact-a-contingency-framework-for-measuring-social-performance/)
- Core claim: "some organizations should measure long-term impacts, while others should focus on shorter-term outputs and outcomes". It is "neither feasible nor desirable" for all organisations to measure impact. — [MandE](https://mande.co.uk/2010/uncategorized/the-limits-of-nonprofit-impact-a-contingency-framework-for-measuring-social-performance/); [Euclid Network](https://knowledgecentre.euclidnetwork.eu/2023/03/16/the-limits-of-nonprofit-impact-a-contingency-framework-for-measuring-social-performance/)
- Alnoor Ebrahim is now at the Fletcher School, Tufts University. — [Tufts](https://fletcher.tufts.edu/people/faculty/alnoor-ebrahim). Tufts lists a related paper, "Putting the brakes on impact: A contingency framework for measuring social performance". — [Tufts](https://fletcher.tufts.edu/research/research-publications/putting-brakes-impact-contingency-framework-measuring-social)
- The original HBS PDF URL (hbs.edu/ris/Publication Files/10-099.pdf) returned 404 when tested, so a stable direct link still needs to be found. Copies are linked from the MandE and artstrategies pages. — [artstrategies](https://www.artstrategies.org/the-limits-of-nonprofit-impact-hbs-working-paper/)

**4. Ruff & Olsen, "The Next Frontier in Social Impact Measurement Isn't Measurement at All" (SSIR, 10 May 2016).**
- Authors are Kate Ruff (Carleton University) and Sara Olsen (SVT Group). They argue that common, standardised measures "ask the wrong questions, measure the wrong things". Organisations should instead measure in context-specific ways, document their methods and stay consistent, so that skilled analysts can interpret and compare reports. The argument rests on an analogy with the history of financial accounting, not on empirical studies. — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/next_frontier_in_social_impact_measurement)

**5. ActKnowledge / Center for Theory of Change (Heléne Clark, Dana Taplin), New York. Free.**
- *Theory of Change Basics: A Primer* (Taplin & Clark, 2012). — [PDF](https://www.theoryofchange.org/wp-content/uploads/toco_library/pdf/ToCBasics.pdf)
  - *Technical Papers* (2013). — [PDF](https://www.theoryofchange.org/wp-content/uploads/toco_library/pdf/ToC-Tech-Papers.pdf)
  - *Facilitator's Source Book* (2012). — [PDF](https://www.theoryofchange.org/wp-content/uploads/toco_library/pdf/ToCFacilitatorSourcebook.pdf)
  - "Scope: How much should a good theory account for?" — [PDF](https://www.theoryofchange.org/wp-content/uploads/toco_library/pdf/scope.pdf)
  - "Making Sense: Reviewing Program Design with ToC". — [PDF](https://www.theoryofchange.org/wp-content/uploads/toco_library/pdf/making_sense.pdf)
  - Library index. — [theoryofchange.org](https://www.theoryofchange.org/library/publications/)
- From the ToC Basics text (checked directly):
  - Process: long-term goal → backwards mapping of preconditions → voicing assumptions and rationales → choosing interventions → indicators → quality review → written narrative.
  - The quality review asks three questions: is the theory "1) plausible, 2) 'doable' (or feasible), and 3) testable?"
  - Rationales "explain the connections between the outcomes and why one outcome is needed to achieve another".
  - Assumptions are conditions "you take for granted", meaning they already exist. Preconditions, by contrast, must be brought about.
  - Rationales and assumptions are "often … supported by research, strengthening the plausibility of the theory".
  - Indicator formula: each indicator needs four targets, "How many of who or what will reach what level by when?"
  — [PDF](https://www.theoryofchange.org/wp-content/uploads/toco_library/pdf/ToCBasics.pdf)
- ActKnowledge adds a fourth quality criterion, "Appropriate Scope": broad enough to leave no gaps, but focused on the resources at hand. Testability refers mainly to the indicators. — [ALNAP copy of ToC Tech Papers](https://library.alnap.org/system/files/content/resource/files/main/toc-tech-papers.pdf) (search snippet)

**6. Urban Institute & The Center for What Works, Outcome Indicators Project. Free.**
- The project provides "candidate outcomes and outcome indicators" for nonprofits building or improving outcome monitoring. It covers 14 program areas. Each area has a sample mission statement, an outcome sequence chart, a table of candidate outcomes and indicators, and data collection strategies with suggested data sources per indicator. — [Urban Institute project page](https://www.urban.org/policy-centers/cross-center-initiatives/performance-management-measurement/projects/nonprofit-organizations/projects-focused-nonprofit-organizations/outcome-indicators-project) (403 to fetcher; content from search snippet)
- "Building a Common Outcome Framework to Measure Nonprofit Performance" extends the idea to all nonprofit programmes. It sorts outcomes into four areas: program-centred, participant-centred, community-centred and organisation-centred. — [PDF via BetterEvaluation](https://betterevaluation.org/sites/default/files/411404-Building-a-Common-Outcome-Framework-To-Measure-Nonprofit-Performance.PDF) (403 to fetcher; content from search snippet)

**7. W.K. Kellogg Foundation (a foundation, not academic). Free.**
- *Logic Model Development Guide* (updated January 2004). It defines the logic model as linking outcomes with activities and "the theoretical assumptions/principles of the program". — [PDF (wkkf issuelab)](https://wkkf.issuelab.org/resources/10124/10124.pdf); [mirror](https://www.naccho.org/uploads/downloadable-resources/Programs/Public-Health-Infrastructure/KelloggLogicModelGuide_161122_162808.pdf); [BetterEvaluation](https://www.betterevaluation.org/tools-resources/wk-kellogg-foundation-logic-model-guide)
- *Evaluation Handbook* (1998, revised 2014) has been succeeded by *The Step-by-Step Guide to Evaluation: How to Become Savvy Evaluation Consumers* (November 2017), aimed at "grantees, nonprofits and community leaders". It is free for non-commercial use. — [issuelab](https://wkkf.issuelab.org/resource/the-step-by-step-guide-to-evaluation-how-to-become-savvy-evaluation-consumers-4.html); [PDF mirror (NCFP)](https://www.ncfp.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/The-Step-by-Step-Guide-to-Evaluation-WK-Kellogg-Foundation-2017-the-step-by-step-guide-to-evaluation.pdf); [old Handbook PDF](https://www.public-health.uiowa.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/Kellogg-Foundation-Evaluation-Handbook.pdf)

**8. University of Wisconsin-Extension, Program Development & Evaluation. Free.**
- *Developing a Logic Model: Teaching and Training Guide* (Taylor-Powell & Henert, February 2008). It contains 17 activities with handouts and slides, is aimed at "beginning-level logic model users", and uses the UW-Extension format (Inputs → Outputs → Outcomes short/medium/long, plus assumptions and external factors). — [PDF](https://fyi.extension.wisc.edu/programdevelopment/files/2016/03/lmguidecomplete.pdf); [UWEX Designing programs](https://fyi.extension.wisc.edu/programdevelopment/designing-programs/)

**9. J-PAL (MIT). Free, but RCT-oriented.**
- J-PAL publishes teaching resources from its "Evaluating Social Programs" course. They include lectures on "Why Evaluate" and "Theory of Change and Measurement", the latter covering defining and measuring outcomes, types and sources of data, and measurement error. — [J-PAL teaching resources](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/resource/teaching-resources-randomized-evaluations); [L2 Measurement slides PDF](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/L2_Measurement.pdf)
- "Introduction to measurement and indicators" (Kopper & Parry) gives an overview of measurement concepts, measurement error and common data sources. It sits in the Repository of Measurement and Survey Design Resources. — [J-PAL repository](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/resource/repository-measurement-and-survey-design-resources)

**10. Harvard Business School, Impact-Weighted Accounts (IWA). Free, but for companies and investors.**
- Led by George Serafeim, IWA aims to create financial accounts that reflect social and environmental performance. Impacts are converted into monetary values using a database of 593 valuation coefficients. — [Embedding Project](https://embeddingproject.org/resources/impact-weighted-accounts-project); [Nova SBE](https://roletoplay.novasbe.pt/content/impact-weighted-accounts-can-accounting-shift-the-paradigm)
- The project ended at HBS in 2021. It was spun out in 2022 into the International Foundation for Valuing Impacts (IFVI), which started operating in January 2023. — [Impact Management Platform](https://impactmanagementplatform.org/?p=6263); [Summa Equity](https://summaequity.com/measuring-what-matters-how-impact-accounting-redefines-sustainability-measurement/)

### Inferences
- The SSIR/Gugerty-Karlan line is the strongest US academic backing for "right-sized" measurement. The Goldilocks Toolkit is its free, practical form.
- The ToC Basics primer overlaps heavily with the Aspen facilitator guide (same tradition: backwards mapping, preconditions). Its main new elements are the plausible/doable/testable check and the four-part indicator formula.

- Harvard Kennedy School evaluation resources: not researched in this session. I found no specific HKS guide aimed at small nonprofits.
- Urban Institute: I could not open the PDFs (403). Authors and year of "Building a Common Outcome Framework" are unverified (I recall Lampkin, Hatry et al., about 2006). ## Which of steps 0–6 each source informs, and what it adds to our catalogue

### Takeaway
What is new compared with PHINEO, Aspen and IMP is mainly: (1) explicit rules for what NOT to measure and when not to evaluate impact (CART, Ten Reasons, Ebrahim & Rangan); (2) a 3-question quality check for the ToC (plausible / doable / testable, plus appropriate scope); (3) the four-part indicator formula; (4) the split between assumptions (inside the causal chain) and risks (outside control), with "many assumptions = focus data collection here"; (5) ready-made generic outcome-indicator banks (Urban Institute).

**Labelling the evidence behind assumptions** (we currently use own experience / data / research):
- ActKnowledge treats research support for rationales and assumptions as what makes a theory "plausible". — [ToC Basics](https://www.theoryofchange.org/wp-content/uploads/toco_library/pdf/ToCBasics.pdf)
- Goldilocks recommends collecting data where assumptions are numerous or critical. — [Goldilocks ToC](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/publications/Goldilocks-Toolkit-Theory-of-Change_1.pdf)
- Gugerty & Karlan say to check whether existing evidence applies to your context. — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ten_reasons_not_to_measure_impact_and_what_to_do_instead)
- None of the sources found offers a ready-made simple evidence scale.

### Inferences
- A useful refinement of our label: keep the three labels and add a fourth state, "not yet supported / we're unsure", plus one action rule: "unsure + critical assumption → check it in your data collection plan (step 6)". This combines the Goldilocks prioritisation logic with our existing labels. It is my own synthesis, not a scale from any one source.
- Adding the "plausible / doable / testable?" check as a 3-question end-of-step check for step 3 would cost the user about 5 minutes.
- Adding the CART "Actionable + Responsible" questions as a filter at the end of step 4 (or step 6), e.g. "What will we do differently depending on the result? Is collecting this worth the participants' time?", directly delivers "what NOT to measure".
- The ToC Basics indicator formula is a plain-language alternative to SMART that fits steps 4 and 6.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether Ebrahim & Rangan's "What Impact?" (CMR 2014) adds a simpler typology than the 2010 working paper.
- I did not check whether the Goldilocks Toolkit has a dedicated module on choosing indicators.

## Relevance verdict for a non-expert single user with about 1 hour per week

### Takeaway
Recommended: Gugerty & Karlan "Ten Reasons" (SSIR), the IPA Goldilocks ToC module, ActKnowledge "ToC Basics", and the Urban Institute candidate indicators. Optional: Ebrahim & Rangan as a background principle, and the UW-Extension and Kellogg guides as templates. Not recommended: HBS Impact-Weighted Accounts, J-PAL RCT course material, and paywalled books.

### Cited Findings
- **Recommended, high:** "Ten Reasons" is a free, readable magazine article. CART gives four plain-language tests. — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ten_reasons_not_to_measure_impact_and_what_to_do_instead)
- **Recommended, high:** the Goldilocks ToC module is a short PDF (about 229 KB) with worked examples ("Nutrition for All"). It is free and aimed at organisations of all sizes. — [PDF](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/publications/Goldilocks-Toolkit-Theory-of-Change_1.pdf); [S4YE](https://www.s4ye.org/node/1726)
- **Recommended, medium-high:** ToC Basics is a short, free primer. It is close to Aspen, so use it for the quality check and the indicator formula, not as a separate method. — [PDF](https://www.theoryofchange.org/wp-content/uploads/toco_library/pdf/ToCBasics.pdf)
- **Recommended, medium (step 4 inspiration only):** the Urban Institute indicators. They are sector-specific to US nonprofit types and date from the mid-2000s, but the indicator sequence charts and per-indicator data sources are reusable as patterns. — [Urban](https://www.urban.org/policy-centers/cross-center-initiatives/performance-management-measurement/projects/nonprofit-organizations/projects-focused-nonprofit-organizations/outcome-indicators-project)
- **Optional, medium:** Ebrahim & Rangan is an academic working paper, too long for the user to read. Extract its one principle ("not every organisation should measure impact; match the result level to your control over outcomes") into step 0 or step 3. — [MandE](https://mande.co.uk/2010/uncategorized/the-limits-of-nonprofit-impact-a-contingency-framework-for-measuring-social-performance/)
- **Optional, low-medium:** the UW-Extension guide (a 2008 facilitator kit) and the Kellogg Logic Model Guide (2004). Both are free but long and largely redundant with PHINEO. — [UWEX](https://fyi.extension.wisc.edu/programdevelopment/files/2016/03/lmguidecomplete.pdf); [wkkf](https://wkkf.issuelab.org/resources/10124/10124.pdf)
- **Not recommended:** HBS IWA/IFVI. It monetises corporate impacts using valuation coefficients and is meant for companies and investors. — [Embedding Project](https://embeddingproject.org/resources/impact-weighted-accounts-project)
- **Not recommended, except the measurement-error primer:** J-PAL material, which is built around randomised evaluations. — [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/resource/teaching-resources-randomized-evaluations)
- **Not recommended (principle only):** Ruff & Olsen, a policy-level argument about capital markets. — [SSIR](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/next_frontier_in_social_impact_measurement)

### Inferences
- For the Wirkungshelferin, these sources are best used as built-in prompts and checks rather than reading assignments: a CART filter, the ToC quality questions, an assumption-prioritisation rule and a "how many of who, what level, by when" indicator template. That keeps the user's load within 1 hour per week.

### Gaps
- I did not check the reading length or ease of the Goldilocks book chapters or of the Urban Institute PDFs.
