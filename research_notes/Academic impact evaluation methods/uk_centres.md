# UK academic centres and think tanks: impact measurement methods for small nonprofits

Scope: methods only, mapped to our 7-step guide (0 context, 1 target groups, 2 problem & root causes, 3 ToC incl. assumptions, 4 indicators, 5 data collection methods, 6 data collection plan). Existing catalogue: PHINEO Kursbuch Wirkung, Aspen ToC guide, PHINEO Wirkungsrad, IRIS+/IMP, SRS 2014, SVI Standards 2025 draft, Matatihi SROI guide.

Summary: the most useful UK material is **not** from universities. It comes from NPC (think tank) and Nesta/Project Oracle. The Oxford, Cambridge and LSE centres mostly produce research, investor material, policy work or paid courses. The one exception is the Oxford GO Lab outcomes guide, which is useful for step 4.

## Which centres publish freely available, practical methods that add to our catalogue?

### Takeaway
Four sources are worth adopting:
- **NPC Theory of Change in Ten Steps (2019)**
- **NPC Five Types of Data framework (2020)**
- **Nesta Standards of Evidence (2013)**
- **NPC Understanding Impact (2020)**

The **GO Lab "Setting and measuring outcomes" guide** (Oxford) is a useful secondary source for step 4. Saïd/Skoll, CISL, Cambridge Judge and LSE Marshall have nothing practical and free for a single non-expert user.

### Cited Findings

**1. NPC (New Philanthropy Capital), a think tank and not an academic body. Highly relevant.**
- **Theory of Change in Ten Steps** by James Noble / NPC, 2019, free PDF: https://npproduction.wpenginepowered.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Theory-of-Change-10-Steps-Updated.pdf — [NPC resource page](https://www.thinknpc.org/resource-hub/creating-your-theory-of-change-npcs-practical-guide/)
  - Its ten steps are: (1) situation analysis (problem, causes, context), (2) target groups, (3) impact, (4) outcomes, (5) activities, (6) **change mechanisms**, (7) sequencing, (8) ToC diagram, (9) stakeholders and enabling factors, (10) assumptions. — [NPC](https://www.thinknpc.org/resource-hub/creating-your-theory-of-change-npcs-practical-guide/)
  - It names change "mechanisms" as their own element instead of hiding them in assumptions. It separates a "developmental" ToC (building a strategy) from a "descriptive" one (describing existing work), and it warns against focusing on targets and measurement too early. — [NPC](https://www.thinknpc.org/resource-hub/creating-your-theory-of-change-npcs-practical-guide/)
  - It informs steps 0, 1, 2 and 3. The step order matches ours almost exactly: situation analysis, then target groups, then the ToC.
- **Five Types of Data framework** by Jessica Weir, NPC, about 2020, free PDF (470 KB): https://www.thinknpc.org/resource-hub/five-types-of-data-framework/ — [NPC](https://www.thinknpc.org/resource-hub/five-types-of-data-framework/); explainer blog: [NPC blog](https://www.thinknpc.org/blog/5-types-of-data-for-assessing-your-work-an-explainer/)
  - The five types are:
    - **User data**: who you reach, collected at sign-up
    - **Engagement data**: how often, how long and how well people take part, usually recorded by staff
    - **Feedback data**: what users, staff and volunteers think
    - **Outcomes data**: short-term change in knowledge, attitudes, behaviour or resources; needs follow-up and often gets low response
    - **Impact data**: lasting change; complex, so collect it only when "realistic and proportionate"
    — [NPC](https://www.thinknpc.org/resource-hub/five-types-of-data-framework/)
  - Key guidance for small organisations: "For most organisations, strong user, engagement, and feedback data provides a solid foundation for learning and improvement." Not all five types are needed. — [NPC](https://www.thinknpc.org/resource-hub/five-types-of-data-framework/)
  - It informs steps 4, 5 and 6.
- **Understanding Impact** by James Noble, Peter O'Flynn and Anne Kazimirski, NPC, 12 Feb 2020, free PDF (41 pp). It builds on the Ten Steps and shows how to turn a ToC into a measurement framework. It covers the five data types, how to prioritise, and seven mini-guides (quantitative methods, qualitative data, sampling, evidence standards, research ethics, data analysis, learning culture). — [NPC](https://www.thinknpc.org/resource-hub/understanding-impact/)
  - It informs steps 3 to 6. It replaces the older four pillar approach.
- **Building your measurement framework: NPC's four pillar approach** by Anne Kazimirski and David Pritchard, NPC, June 2014, free (full guide 52 pp; 2-page summary: https://www.thinknpc.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/NPCs-four-pillars-summary.pdf). Its four pillars are: (1) map your theory of change, (2) prioritise what you measure, (3) choose your level of evidence, (4) select your sources and tools (reuse existing tools first). — [NPC](https://www.thinknpc.org/resource-hub/npcs-four-pillar-approach/)
  - NPC says it has since updated this guidance and points readers to "Understanding Impact". — [NPC](https://www.thinknpc.org/resource-hub/npcs-four-pillar-approach/)

**2. Nesta Standards of Evidence (Puttick & Ludlow). Highly relevant as a simple scale.**
- Puttick, R. & Ludlow, J., "Standards of Evidence: an approach that balances the need for evidence with innovation", Nesta, October 2013, free PDF: https://media.nesta.org.uk/documents/standards_of_evidence.pdf — (PDF text read directly). It was adapted from the GLA's Project Oracle standards. — [Nesta PDF](https://media.nesta.org.uk/documents/standards_of_evidence.pdf)
- The five levels, quoted from the PDF:
  - **L1** "You can describe what you do and why it matters, logically, coherently and convincingly"
  - **L2** "You capture data that shows positive change, but you cannot confirm you caused this"
  - **L3** "You can demonstrate causality using a control or comparison group"
  - **L4** "You have one + independent replication evaluations that confirms these conclusions"
  - **L5** "You have manuals, systems and procedures to ensure consistent replication and positive impact"
  — [Nesta PDF](https://media.nesta.org.uk/documents/standards_of_evidence.pdf)
- Level 1 is "a low threshold, appropriate to very early-stage innovations". At this level you "should be able to do this yourself, and draw upon existing data and research from other sources." — [Nesta PDF](https://media.nesta.org.uk/documents/standards_of_evidence.pdf)
- It informs steps 3 and 6 (how strong the evidence needs to be). It frames rigour as proportionate to the project's stage.

**3. Project Oracle (London youth evidence hub; Nesta's standards came from here).**
- Project Oracle described itself as a "Children and Youth Evidence Hub". It offered Theory of Change templates, an Evaluation Toolkit with a self-evaluation template, and five-stage Standards of Evidence. Its Standard 1 is reached through a ToC and an evaluation plan. — [London.gov.uk LSEF resources](https://www.london.gov.uk/programmes-strategies/education-and-youth/education-and-youth-publications/lsef-teaching-resources/programme) (via search snippet; the page returned 403 when fetched); see also [IES evaluation of Project Oracle](https://www.employment-studies.co.uk/resource/resource-evaluation-project-oracle/)

**4. University of Oxford: Government Outcomes Lab (GO Lab), Blavatnik School.**
- **Setting and measuring outcomes** (technical guidance), by Grace Young, updated by Andreea Anastasiu. It is a web guide marked "beta", last updated 2 Dec 2020, with a Creative Commons licence noted in the site footer. URL: https://golab.bsg.ox.ac.uk/toolkit/technical-guidance/setting-measuring-outcomes/ — [GO Lab](https://golab.bsg.ox.ac.uk/toolkit/technical-guidance/setting-measuring-outcomes/); list of technical guides: [GO Lab](https://golab.bsg.ox.ac.uk/toolkit/technical-guidance/)
  - Its sections cover: why measure outcomes; outcomes frameworks (outcome, measure, target); identifying outcomes (**primary/secondary, hard/soft, proxy, lead/progression outcomes**); measuring them (cohort vs. individual); and setting targets. Targets cover achievability, **perverse incentives ("cherry picking", "creaming/parking")** and external factors. The guide warns against outcomes "unduly influenced by factors outside the control of the provider". — [GO Lab](https://golab.bsg.ox.ac.uk/toolkit/technical-guidance/setting-measuring-outcomes/)
  - It informs step 4 (and partly step 6).
- **Impact Wayfinder [Prototype]** is a free online five-step filter: sector, type of impact, project stage, scale, and output format (qualitative / quantitative / monetary). It returns a list of evaluation approaches (203 in the prototype). — [GO Lab](https://golab.bsg.ox.ac.uk/toolkit/wayfinder-assessment-resources/)
- Its other toolkit items (Impact Bond Lifecycle, Pricing Outcomes, Awarding contracts, GO Lab–CIPFA Value for Money toolkit, SIB Provider Toolkit) are aimed at commissioners and impact-bond contracting. — [GO Lab toolkit](https://golab.bsg.ox.ac.uk/toolkit/)

### Inferences
- Value against our catalogue, by step:
  - **Step 3 (ToC):** NPC's explicit **"change mechanisms"** element is what PHINEO and Aspen lack. Aspen links outcomes through preconditions, but nowhere asks plainly "*why/how* does this activity cause this change?". A one-line "because…" field per arrow would be cheap to add. NPC's "stakeholders & enabling factors" step overlaps with Aspen's assumptions but makes external dependencies concrete.
  - **Step 4 (indicators):** GO Lab adds **proxy and lead/progression outcomes** (intermediate markers you can measure inside a short project) and a warning about **perverse incentives** (choosing indicators that reward working only with easy participants). Neither is explicit in PHINEO's SMART guidance.
  - **Steps 5–6 (data):** NPC's **Five Types of Data** is the strongest addition. It gives a non-expert a ready order of priority: start with user, engagement and feedback data, and add outcome data where feasible. It explicitly allows skipping impact data. This fits a user with about 1 hour a week. IMP's 15 data categories and PHINEO's method overview don't offer this ordering.
  - **Evidence scale:** Nesta's 5 levels rate the **whole intervention**, not single assumptions. Our current label for an assumption's evidence base (own experience / data / research) still fits the assumption level better. An optional project-level badge could reuse Nesta L1/L2 in plain language: L1 = "we can explain logically why it works, drawing on existing research"; L2 = "we collect data showing change, but cannot prove we caused it". L3+ needs comparison groups and is out of reach for our users. Showing L1→L2 would set realistic expectations. Nesta's L1 wording also implies a fourth source worth adding to our assumption labels: "**existing research by others**" as distinct from "own data" (we already have "research", so this is consistent).
- **Verdicts for a single non-expert user with about 1 hour a week:**
  - **Recommended:**
    - NPC Five Types of Data: short, plain language, explicitly proportionate
    - NPC ToC in Ten Steps: plain language; maps onto steps 0–3
    - Nesta Standards of Evidence: about 5 pages; use only Levels 1–2
  - **Selectively recommended:** NPC Understanding Impact (41 pp; the mini-guides on sampling and ethics are useful as reference); GO Lab Setting & Measuring Outcomes (written for commissioners and impact bonds, but the outcome taxonomy and perverse-incentive section transfer well).
  - **Superseded / historical:** NPC four pillars (2014), replaced by Understanding Impact; Project Oracle materials (status unclear).
  - **Not recommended:**
    - GO Lab pricing / VfM / SIB toolkits: written for commissioners and payment-by-results
    - GO Lab Impact Wayfinder: a 200+ item list of methods overwhelms a non-expert
    - Oxford Saïd / Skoll / Impact Measurement Programme: paid executive course and academic research
    - CISL Cambridge Impact Framework: an SDG portfolio framework for investors
    - Cambridge Judge CCSI and LSE Marshall: no practical free guide found
    - Magenta Book: not checked; it targets government evaluators and is too heavy for this user
