# Log step 5 – Measurement focus & indicators

## Critique (draft → final)
1. **Too thin:** no question guide for choosing the focus, no example, no indicator wording rule. → Fully worked out.
2. **No priority order for choosing the focus:** without help, a layperson picks the biggest question ("Do we reduce …?"). → NPC Five Types (S11) as a ladder: reach, participation, feedback first; outcomes where they matter; impact only when realistic. Plus the "Actionable" test from CART (S12): "Was würdet ihr anders machen, wenn …?"
3. **Level 7 with indicators?** The draft didn't rule it out. S1 shows with PAFF that a target value at society level is not meaningful (p. 68), and S1 p. 83–84 names impact evidence as hard. → No indicators for level 7; rubric ⚠ if anyway.
4. **Indicator ≠ instrument, and indicator at the right point in the chain:** the most common beginner mistakes (S2 p. 14). → Pitfalls + rubric.
5. **Max. 2 per question vs. "about 3 per goal" (S3 p. 28):** apparent contradiction. → Explained: our limit is a deliberate right-sizing decision (PRD F5, S8 proportionate rigour); S3 refers to complete goal sets.
6. **Baseline unrealistic for a running project:** the draft just said "baseline". → Retrospective baseline (self-assessment before/now on a scale) and "set a new baseline" (S1 p. 66); target values from experience, comparable projects, baseline – "felt" values are allowed at the start (S1 p. 67).
7. **Source issue CART:** the Goldilocks PDF only names CART (p. 5); the explanation is in the SSIR article (not saved). → Cited as "S12 (SSIR-Artikel)".

## Verification
- Does S1 describe a retrospective baseline and setting a new baseline? **Yes.** Kursbuch p. 66 (margin box).
- Does S2 separate indicator and measurement instrument, and warn against indicators at the wrong point in the chain? **Yes.** ToC guide p. 14.
- Does NPC (S11) say impact data should only be collected when realistic, proportionate and meaningful? **Yes.** Five Types of Data, p. 1.
