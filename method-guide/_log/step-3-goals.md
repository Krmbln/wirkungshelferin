# Log step 3 – Impact goals

## Critique (draft → final)
1. **Unclear what a level-6 goal links to:** in the S1 example, the target-group goal at the life-situation level is the inverted core problem (PAFF "Jugendliche bekommen einen Ausbildungsplatz", p. 29/30). Levels 4–5 come from the inverted causes. The draft only said "linked to a cause". → Rule: level 6 = inverted core problem, linked to the causes it mainly resolves (consistent with PRD F5 "≥ 1 root cause"); levels 4–5 = inverted causes.
2. **No assignment help for levels:** a layperson mixes knowledge, behaviour and situation. → Verb test: "weiß/kann/traut sich" (4), "tut" (5), "Lage hat sich verändert" (6).
3. **"Messbar" in SMART creates pressure too early:** indicators only come in step 5, and S3 p. 22 says that at higher levels precise quantification is often hard. → Rubric demands "checkable in principle" (S1 checklist p. 32), not a number.
4. **Non-influenceable causes:** the draft didn't say whether they become goals. → No goal; the choice of approach (S1 p. 31) decides which causes the project tackles.
5. **Order of working:** S1 p. 28 advises focusing first on the central goal at the life-situation level. → Order 6 → 5 → 4, level 7 at the end.
6. **Vague "mega goals":** Aspen (S2 p. 11) warns against big, multidimensional goals without unpacking. → Pitfall + reaction.
7. **Blockers missing:** PRD hard rules (≥ 1 goal per level 4–6 or "not applicable" with Why; ≥ 1 goal per non-focus group; one level-7 statement; Why) → ⛔.
8. **Participation:** S1 p. 27 (margin) says to develop goals with the target groups. → Optional prompt.

Level test extended with "Lage" examples and a warning ("liest sicher" = level 4).
